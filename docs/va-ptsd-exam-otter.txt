RUNNING TRANSCRIPT
------------------------------------------------------------

[…] a Special Forces Unit from June, 1963, to January, 1964. During his tour a
[numbe]r of men were killed and injured. He stated that his mission was considered
[danger]ous, deadly and lethal and he states that he has many classic symptoms of PTSD,
[He extreme]ly distrusts people and has kept a loaded weapon nearby as a constant protection
[…] the military and he knew this was ingrained from his Vietnam experience. He was
[not] disturbed so much because of what happened to him or what he went through but
what was required of him in order to complete his mission, which meant the mission of
being deadly and lethal as part of the Special Forces. He gave no detail, he just made
inferences. He stated that he did not get along with his boss and was concerned that he
would be the first to go in the next cut and that they have had several in the last couple of
years. He was employed at Republic Steel and said as we talked that they were
considering closing and this disturbed him quite a bit. He also stated that he had severe
nightmares and has had them since the war. He awakened screaming, often jumping up,
and this was reported to him by his former wife, sitting up, having a cold sweat and
sometimes could not remember if he was conscious. All of those things are told to him.
If he awakened from one of the dreams or nightmare then he would remember. He stated
that there were certain sights and sounds that just made him extremely nervous and he
would begin to tremble. He relayed another incident where a soldier had a pet otter and
that the otter bit him and he could not find his gun. The otter was in the ground, like in a
ditch of some kind, and he went and got a C4 and blew up the otter. This earned him the
nickname "Otter." He stated he became very annoyed by persons who falsely claim to
have been in Vietnam and tells stories when he knew that they were never there. He also
described his job situation as bothering him because he stated that he had worked a very
responsible labor job and he was an overseer for three to four job sites and he would
coordinate and control these sites. His job was changed recently and on his current job he
simply sat and answered phones and gave tickets to the driver. He had less control and it
was more just like traffic control. He was kind of concerned about his job situation and
he also had conflict with his supervisor and he was concerned about job loss because
there was talk of closing the steel mill. He also stated that the recent incident of the
Towers affected him and he stated the rubble, the smoke, the attack truly bothered him of
the Trade Center with many being killed and injured. He felt it was unbelievable and
made him angry, sick, nauseous, bitter and aggressive and his attitude was "go get those
guys." In terms of other incidences he just would always repeat that he was in the Special
Forces and there was a lot of death around him, but he was not believed able to talk about
the incidences and specifically told the examiner that "nobody wants to talk about that."
He did have recurrent distressing dreams, nightmares about the war. He described them
as one to three times per month, as he could recall them, but he thought he was having
them and he did not recall them. He only remembered them if he was awakened. He had
flashbacks where he felt like he was immediately in the situation back there and it seemed
real to him. There were certain things that he could not tolerate that reminded him of the
war. He no longer hunted during hunting season. He could no longer shoot innocent
animals. He stated that he could watch war movies without too much distress but he did
have reactivity to loud noises, gunfire, fireworks. July 4th was taken off his calendar
because it made him nervous and he needed to escape the sights and the sounds. He did
not talk about the war to others and he never had unless it was a comrade from Vietnam.

STRUCTURED EXTRACT — "OTTER" INCIDENT (examiner notes)
============================================================

Setting given on this page: Vietnam (no camp, province, or calendar date
beyond the tour window June 1963 – January 1964). Do not invent a place.

As told to the examiner:
  - Another soldier had a pet otter.
  - The otter bit him (Paul).
  - He could not find his gun.
  - The otter was "in the ground, like in a ditch of some kind."
  - He got C4 and blew up the otter.
  - This earned him the nickname "Otter."

This is an examiner paraphrase of what Paul relayed during a VA PTSD
exam. It is not an after-action report and does not name a camp.

Family oral history (separate source) is another recollection of the
same incident: tossed explosives into a latrine and killed an otter.
One event; two tellings. Detail differs (latrine vs. pet otter / ditch /
C4); do not treat them as different incidents.
